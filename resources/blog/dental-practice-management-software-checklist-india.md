Choosing software for a dental clinic is a decision you will live with every day, so it is worth doing carefully. This checklist is written for clinics in India and focuses on what actually matters in daily work. Use it to compare any product, including ours.

## Start with your own workflow

Before looking at any product, write down what happens in a normal day: how patients book, who confirms the visit, where the patient's history is kept, how the bill is made, and how you know what the clinic earned this month. The best software is the one that makes those specific steps shorter, not the one with the longest feature list.

## The core features to expect

**Appointments.** You should be able to book a visit against a patient, a service and a doctor, see the day's schedule at a glance, and mark each visit as confirmed, completed or cancelled. Ask how confirmations and reminders work, and whether messages are sent for you or prepared for you to send.

**Patient records.** At a minimum: contact details, date of birth, blood group, allergies, notes, and a history of visits, invoices and prescriptions. Check that allergies are easy to see, not buried.

**Billing.** Look for invoices built from a service list, automatic numbering, discount and tax fields, paid and unpaid tracking, and a printable PDF that carries your clinic details.

**Prescriptions.** Structured medicines with dose, frequency and duration, printable for the patient.

**Treatment plans.** If you do root canals, implants or orthodontics, you need a way to track treatments that take several visits.

**Basic reporting.** Monthly revenue, new patients and your most-performed treatments should be one click away, not a spreadsheet project.

## What matters specifically in India

- **GST and invoices.** The software should let you add a tax percentage and print your GSTIN if you are registered. It is fine if it does not file returns, but it should be clear about what it does and does not do.
- **Hindi or regional-language support.** Front-desk staff work faster in the language they think in.
- **WhatsApp-friendly.** Patients in India live on WhatsApp. Look for confirmations and reminders that fit that habit.
- **Works on ordinary devices.** A browser-based tool that runs on a phone, a tablet and an older computer beats one that needs a specific machine.
- **Pricing in rupees, paid the Indian way.** Check whether you can pay by UPI or bank transfer, and whether there is a plan you can start on for free.

## Privacy and security

Patient data is sensitive, so ask direct questions:

- Is each clinic's data kept separate from every other clinic's?
- Does every staff member get their own login, and can you switch one off when they leave?
- Are passwords stored hashed rather than in plain text?
- Who at the vendor can open your account, and is that visible to you?
- What happens to your data if you stop using the product?

## Ease of use

The test is simple: can a new receptionist book an appointment and make an invoice after an hour of practice? Try the product with real examples from your clinic, not the vendor's demo data. If setup needs a long training session, that cost is yours.

## Support

Find out how you reach a human and how quickly they reply. For a small clinic, being able to message someone on WhatsApp during working hours is often worth more than a long list of features.

## Pricing and commitments

- Is there a permanent free plan, or only a short trial?
- Is pricing per clinic or per doctor?
- Are there setup fees or lock-in contracts?
- Can you upgrade and downgrade freely?

## Questions to ask any vendor

1. Show me how a first-time patient is booked, seen, billed and given a prescription.
2. What can I do on the free or entry plan, and what is locked?
3. How are reminders sent, and by whom?
4. Where is my data kept, and who can access it?
5. Can I leave whenever I like, and what happens to my data?
