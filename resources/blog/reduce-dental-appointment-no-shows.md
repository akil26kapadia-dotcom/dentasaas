Every missed appointment is an empty chair, a doctor waiting, and income that does not come back. No-shows will never reach zero, but a handful of small habits can make them much rarer. Here are eight that work in real clinics, and none of them needs expensive tools.

## 1. Confirm every booking straight away

The moment a patient books, send a confirmation with the essentials: the patient's name, the date and time, the doctor, the treatment and the clinic's address or location link. It takes ten seconds and does two jobs at once. It catches wrong dates and wrong numbers immediately, and it gives the patient something to look back at.

## 2. Remind patients the day before

A short reminder the day before is the single most effective habit. Patients forget: a booking made two weeks ago competes with everything else in their life. Send the reminder in the afternoon or evening so there is still time to reschedule. For early-morning slots, a second reminder that morning helps.

## 3. Make it easy to cancel or reschedule

Patients who cannot easily cancel often just do not turn up. Say plainly that you would rather hear about a change than not see them, and give one number they can message on WhatsApp. When someone cancels, offer a new slot in the same message so the appointment is moved rather than lost.

## 4. Get the contact details right at booking

A reminder is useless if it goes to the wrong number. Check the mobile number when you book, and ask whether it is on WhatsApp. If a patient has no WhatsApp, note that so the front desk knows to call instead.

## 5. Explain how long the visit will take

Long procedures such as root canals need a longer block of the doctor's time. Tell the patient how long the slot is and that it has been reserved for them. People are more likely to keep an appointment when they understand that it cost the clinic something to hold it.

## 6. Consider a clear cancellation policy

For long or expensive procedures, some clinics take a small advance or state a cancellation notice period. If you do, write it down, tell patients when they book, and apply it consistently. Use your judgement here: a policy that feels fair builds trust, while one that feels punishing does the opposite.

## 7. Keep a short waiting list

Some cancellations will still happen. Keep a short list of patients who would take an earlier slot, and message them when one opens up. A cancelled slot that is refilled the same day costs you nothing.

## 8. Look at the pattern

If no-shows cluster on a particular day, time or treatment, that tells you where to focus. Compare how many appointments end as completed and how many as cancelled. Even a rough monthly count shows whether your habits are working.
