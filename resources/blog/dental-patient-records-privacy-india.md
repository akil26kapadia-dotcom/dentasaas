A dental record contains more than teeth. It has a patient's name, phone number, date of birth, address, allergies, medical history and what they paid. Losing control of that information can harm the patient and the clinic's reputation. Here is a practical guide for clinic owners in India. It is general information, not legal advice.

## Simple habits that make the biggest difference

**Give everyone their own login.** A shared password means you cannot tell who saw or changed a record, and you cannot remove one person's access without changing it for everyone. Individual accounts fix both problems.

**Use strong, private passwords.** Long and unique beats short and clever. Do not write them on a sticky note next to the monitor, and do not reuse the password from a personal email account.

**Give people only the access they need.** A receptionist who books appointments does not need the same permissions as the clinic owner. Where your software offers roles, use them.

**Remove access the day someone leaves.** Ex-employees are a common and avoidable risk. Switch the account off on their last day.

**Lock the screen.** The front-desk computer is visible to waiting patients. Lock it when you step away.

**Keep patient information off casual channels.** Do not paste records into large WhatsApp groups or send them through personal accounts. Share only what is needed, with the person who needs it.

**Print less.** Every printout is a copy you now have to protect. Print what the patient needs, and no more.

**Keep devices and browsers updated.** Old software has known security holes. Updates close them.

**Back up, and know how to restore.** A backup you have never tried to restore is a hope, not a plan.

## What the DPDP Act means in plain language

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (the DPDP Act) sets rules for how digital personal data is collected and handled. Its rules and timelines are being brought in in stages, so please check the current position and take professional advice for your clinic. In broad terms, the ideas that matter to a clinic are:

- **Tell people and ask properly.** Patients should know what data you collect and why, and you should collect only what you need for that purpose.
- **Protect it.** You are expected to use reasonable safeguards against loss and misuse.
- **Respect people's rights.** Patients can ask to see, correct or ask you to erase their personal data, subject to what the law allows you to keep.
- **Be ready if something goes wrong.** If personal data is breached, there are obligations to act and to report.

Whoever decides why and how patient data is used, which is usually the clinic, carries the main responsibility. Software vendors that only store and process data on your behalf have their own duties, but they do not remove yours.

## Questions to ask your software vendor

- Is my clinic's data kept separate from other clinics' data?
- Does each of my staff members get their own login?
- Are passwords stored hashed?
- Who at your company can open my account, and will I be able to see when they do?
- How do I request a copy of my data, or its deletion, if I stop using the service?

## A short checklist to start with

1. Everyone has an individual login.
2. Passwords are long, unique and not shared.
3. Roles limit what each person can see.
4. Leavers lose access the same day.
5. Screens are locked when unattended.
6. Patient records are not shared through casual channels.
7. You know where your data is stored and who can reach it.
